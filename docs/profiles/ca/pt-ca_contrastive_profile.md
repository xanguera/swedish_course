# L1 → L2 Contrastive Profile — Portuguese → Catalan (`pt`→`ca`)

> What depends on this specific pair: transfer, interference, and how to phrase meanings. Read
> alongside `L2_ca_language_profile.md`. Step-1 output; consumed by Step 2.

---

## 1. L1 identity & variant
- **L1 name (English):** Portuguese
- **Endonym:** português
- **BCP-47 / ISO code:** `pt` (European, `pt-PT`)
- **Regional variant & why:** **European Portuguese** — *obrigado/obrigada*, *autocarro*, *comboio*,
  *pequeno-almoço*, *casa de banho*, *bilhete*, *se faz favor / por favor*. Chosen for a European
  audience; distinguish deliberately from Brazilian Portuguese.
- **Flag / emoji:** 🇵🇹
- **Typical audience:** European-Portuguese speakers visiting Barcelona.

## 2. Motivation & framing for this audience
- **Why it's worth it:** Catalan and Portuguese are both Western Romance languages with a huge shared
  Latin core, so Catalan feels **familiar fast**. Crucially, European Portuguese already **reduces
  unstressed vowels and uses a schwa** — the single hardest thing about Catalan for most learners is
  something a PT speaker does natively.
- **Difficulty:** **Easy → Medium.** Vocabulary and pronunciation transfer strongly; the real work is
  Catalan-specific *spelling* (`ll`, `ny`, `l·l`), the article/contraction *forms*, and a handful of
  false friends.
- **The honest pitch (onboarding):** "Já falas uma língua prima — o catalão vai parecer-te familiar. E
  em Barcelona, as pessoas adoram quando um visitante fala em catalão."

## 3. Cognates & positive transfer (easy wins)
- **Near-identical high-frequency cognates** (CA ↔ PT):
  *bon dia ↔ bom dia*, *bona nit ↔ boa noite*, *adéu ↔ adeus*, *sí/no ↔ sim/não*, *aigua ↔ água*,
  *pa ↔ pão*, *vi ↔ vinho*, *llet ↔ leite*, *plaça ↔ praça*, *mercat ↔ mercado*, *nom ↔ nome*,
  *nit ↔ noite*, *dia ↔ dia*, *home ↔ homem*, *gat ↔ gato*, *temps ↔ tempo*, *beure ↔ beber*,
  *dormir ↔ dormir*, *estació ↔ estação*.
  **Numbers:** *un↔um, dos↔dois, tres↔três, quatre↔quatro, cinc↔cinco, sis↔seis, set↔sete, nou↔nove,
  deu↔dez* (only *vuit↔oito* diverges).
- **Shared grammar the learner already "gets"** (→ assume, see §6): grammatical gender; definite /
  indefinite articles; adjective agreement; rich verb conjugation; the **T–V distinction** (*tu* vs
  *você / o senhor* ↔ *tu* vs *vostè*); and **prep+article contractions** — PT's *do/da/ao/pelo* map
  directly onto Catalan *del/de la/al/pel*, so the *concept* needs no teaching, only the forms.
- **Shared sounds (don't over-teach):** schwa & vowel reduction ✓✓, **[ʒ]** (*j/g*) ✓, **[ʃ]** (*x*) ✓,
  **[ʎ]** (CA `ll` = PT `lh`) ✓, **[ɲ]** (CA `ny` = PT `nh`) ✓, voiced/voiceless *s/z* ✓. The sounds are
  familiar; mainly the **spelling** of them is new.

## 4. False friends & interference (traps)
| CA word | looks like PT | CA actually means | the trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cama** | *cama* (bed) | **leg** | biggest trap: CA bed = *llit* (~PT *leito*) |
| **dona** | *dona* (Mrs/lady) | **woman / wife** | |
| **vas** | *vaso* (vase) | **(drinking) glass** | CA also uses *got* for glass |
| **cotxe** | *coche* (old carriage) | **car** | |
| **any** | (English "any"/PT *ano*) | **year** | spelling `ny`=[ɲ], not a false friend but looks alien |
| **pis** | *piso* (floor) | **flat / apartment** | close-but-different |

- **Interference from PT habits to correct:**
  - Reflex to say **obrigado** — must learn **gràcies**; and **por favor / se faz favor** → **si us plau**.
  - **Spelling swaps:** PT `lh/nh` → CA `ll/ny` (*filho→fill*, *senhor→senyor*); PT nasal *ão/-ção* →
    CA `-a` / `-ció` (*pão→pa*, *não→no*, *estação→estació*, *atenção→atenció*).
  - PT **nasal vowels** don't exist in Catalan — resist nasalizing.
  - Final unstressed *-o*→[u] transfers fine, but Catalan masculine nouns often end in a **consonant**
    (*gat*, *pa*, *vi*) where PT keeps a vowel (*gato*, *pão*, *vinho*).

## 5. Phonological contrast (what's hard *for a PT speaker*)
- **Genuinely new/hard:** very little at the sound level. Watch: the **`l·l`** grapheme (sound ≈ plain
  [l], but new spelling); the **`x`** ambiguity ([ʃ] vs [ks]); final consonant clusters (*vint*,
  *cinc* [siŋ]); and open/closed *è/ò* whose distribution differs from Portuguese.
- **Already easy (do not over-teach):** schwa/vowel reduction, [ʒ], [ʃ], [ʎ], [ɲ], s/z voicing.
- **→ Ear-training focus:** not new phonemes but **distinguishing Catalan from the Spanish** the
  learner is simultaneously immersed in (*gràcies* vs *gracias*, *si us plau* vs *por favor*, *sortida*
  vs *salida*). Keep dedicated minimal-pair phoneme drills minimal.

## 6. Grammar contrast (new vs familiar)
| L2 feature | Familiar from PT? | Teaching stance |
|---|---|---|
| Noun gender | **Yes** (m/f) | assume; light touch only on the article forms |
| Articles & contractions | **Concept yes** (*ao/do/pelo*) | teach the **Catalan forms** *el/la, al/del/pel* |
| Formality (T–V) | **Yes** (*tu / você / o senhor*) | light; note *vostè* takes 3rd-person like *você* |
| Question word order | **Yes** | assume; just teach the question **words** |
| Pronouns | **Yes** | teach the set; weak pronouns only as fixed phrases |
- **Net light-grammar picks for this pair:** because gender, contractions and T–V are already familiar,
  spotlight only the **Catalan article/contraction forms** and the **question words**. Treat `ll/ny/l·l`
  as **pronunciation/spelling notes**, not grammar.

## 7. Orthographic contrast
- **Shared (no friction):** acute accents *á é í ó ú*-style marks, and **`ç`**.
- **New / different:** **`l·l`** (*punt volat* — entirely new); **`è ò`** (grave on *e/o*); **`ï ü`**
  (diaeresis — PT dropped the trema); CA **`ll`** where PT writes **`lh`**, CA **`ny`** where PT writes
  **`nh`**; apostrophe elisions *l' d' m'* (PT rarely elides). Enclitic hyphens (*dóna'm*, *anem-hi*) are
  loosely familiar from PT *dá-me*.
- **Same-looking, different-sounding:** *`any`* (looks English; = [aɲ] "year"), *`ll`* (not a doubled *l*).

## 8. Translation & register guidance (how to write `t` and `note`)
- **Write meanings in European Portuguese:** *obrigado/obrigada, autocarro, comboio, pequeno-almoço,
  casa de banho, bilhete, chávena, se faz favor / por favor*.
- **Register:** use **tu** in examples by default; gloss *vostè* as "≈ *você / o senhor*".
- **Useful PT anchors in notes (leverage the cousin language):**
  - *tallat* ≈ Portuguese **garoto/pingo**; *cafè amb llet* ≈ **meia de leite / galão**.
  - Pronunciation notes may use PT analogies: CA `ll` = "como *lh* português", `ny` = "como *nh*",
    `x` = "como *ch* / *x* de *xisto*", schwa = "vogal reduzida, como o *e* final do português".

## 9. Examples localization
- **Sample origin** in template sentences: *Sóc de Portugal* / *Vinc de Lisboa*.
- **Culture cards that land through a PT lens:**
  - ***Sant Joan*** ↔ Portuguese **São João / Santos Populares** (Porto, 23–24 Jun) — bonfires and
    firecrackers: a strong, direct hook. Call it out in that card for the PT audience.
  - *Vermut* ↔ Portuguese *vermute* aperitif habit — familiar.
  - *Sant Jordi* (roses + books) has no direct PT parallel — explain it fresh.

## 10. Distractor opportunities
- **Deliberate hard distractors** from §3–4 near-cognates: *cama* (leg) vs *llit* (bed); *dona* (woman)
  vs the PT sense; *got/vas* (glass); article forms *el/la/els/les*; contractions *al/del/pel*;
  numbers *vuit/nou/deu*.
- **Tagging:** rely on category tags for the auto-distractor pool — put body parts together (so *cama*
  sits with other body parts), drinks together (*tallat/cafè/canya/vi*), so the confusable siblings
  naturally become each other's distractors.

## 11. Immersion / praise strings (keep untranslated in Catalan)
- *Molt bé!* (main "Great!"), *Perfecte!*, *Genial!*, *Endavant!*, *Visca!*.
